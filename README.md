# Calories Calculator
<img width="323" height="685" alt="onboarding" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b4907a27-c74e-4082-ac1c-45751814aa05" />


A mobile app concept designed to make calorie calculation and recipe discovery simple, visual, and approachable.

The project focuses on two core user needs:

- calculating calories and macronutrients for a specific product or a complete dish;
- finding recipes based on personal preferences and nutritional goals.

The main challenge was to present nutritional information clearly without making the product feel clinical, restrictive, or like a traditional fitness tracker.

01 — Branding / Stylescapes

The creative direction was built around the idea of making healthy eating feel fresh, positive, and effortless.

The visual identity combines fresh food photography, expressive typography, a clean color palette, soft shapes, and generous whitespace. Food is intentionally the main visual focus, while nutritional data supports the experience rather than dominating it.

The Stylescape was created to establish the overall visual language of the product before moving into interface design. It brings together the brand mood, typography, colors, photography, and graphic elements into one consistent direction.

02 — Design System
The Design System translates the approved visual direction into a clear and consistent foundation for the product.

It defines a functional color system, typography, spacing, shapes, borders, elevation, iconography, touch targets, and a mobile layout grid. Each element has a specific role and is designed to stay consistent across the interface.

Special attention was given to nutritional data hierarchy — calories are immediately prominent, while macros and supporting information remain easy to scan. Accessibility, contrast, and minimum touch targets were also considered to keep the interface clear and usable.

The system is intentionally simple and lightweight, supporting the product without adding unnecessary visual complexity.

03 — Key Design Screens / Key Flows

The final screens demonstrate how the visual direction and Design System translate into the actual product experience.

Two core user stories were explored.


Flow 01 — Calculate Calories

Product / Dish / AI Food Scan → Nutrition Result

Users can calculate calories for a specific product, build a complete dish from multiple ingredients, or scan food using AI.

The goal was to make calorie calculation fast and intuitive while keeping nutritional information easy to understand.

<img width="324" height="673" alt="mobileflow1" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/304cbbc4-6f1f-4502-ad7f-c78a1d9bfd67" />

AI Food Scan

AI scanning provides a faster way to estimate the nutritional value of a complete meal.

The app identifies ingredients and estimates calories and macronutrients from the image. Because food recognition and portion estimation cannot always be exact, users can review and adjust detected ingredients and quantities before confirming the result.

This provides the convenience of AI while keeping the user in control of the final data

Flow 02 — Discover Recipes

Set Preferences → Recipe Results → Recipe Details

Instead of asking users to browse through an endless recipe catalog, the experience starts with their needs.

Users can select preferences such as meal type, calorie limit, nutritional goal, diet, and cooking time. The app then uses these preferences to surface more relevant recipes.

Recipe cards prioritize the information needed for a quick decision — food photography, calories, protein, cooking time, and relevant tags.

AI-Native Design Process

The project was developed as a step-by-step AI-native workflow:

UX Structure → Branding → Stylescape → Design System → Key Screens → Refinement

AI was used throughout the process to explore ideas, structure the system, and accelerate iteration.

Rather than generating the entire product at once, each stage was reviewed and refined before moving to the next one. This helped maintain consistency between the creative direction, Design System, and final screens.

The final decisions were guided by the original user stories, usability, visual consistency, and product scope.
